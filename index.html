AIRES: an ambulance that re-routes itself

AIRES (AI Intelligent Response & Emergency System) is a student-built emergency response platform. When a road is blocked, it re-routes the ambulance live and updates the driver and the hospital together.

What you see: a disruption is injected from the control panel, AIRES re-routes the ambulance, and the driver screen and hospital dashboard update at the same time.

The problem

Ambulances lose minutes to blocked roads, accidents and the wrong hospital choice. Drivers and hospitals usually find out late and separately.

What AIRES does

It picks a suitable hospital, plans a route over real road data, and keeps checking the route while the ambulance moves. If an event blocks the path, it plans a new one and pushes it to every screen over WebSockets.

Where the project stands

  • Built Dispatch logic, hospital recommendation, live re-routing demo over real Trichy roads, three demo screens (driver, hospital, control panel).
  • In development Live GPS tracking and fuller dashboards.
  • Planned CCTV and traffic-signal integration, voice call intake, PostgreSQL, Docker deployment.

In the demo, traffic, CCTV and weather events are simulated from a control panel. They are not live feeds.

Simulation result

25% less travel time

In 300 simulated runs with a blocked road on the planned route, the average trip took 7.77 minutes without re-routing and 5.86 minutes with AIRES. AIRES was never slower.

This is a simulation result from the project's test scripts, not a real-world trial.
